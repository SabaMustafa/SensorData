Why it matters

Hive inspection is still done by hand

Bees pollinate about 75% of the world's leading food crops. Colony losses from Varroa, disease and climate stress are rising. Yet beekeepers still check colony health by opening the hive, frame by frame.

That is slow, it disturbs the colony, and it does not scale to hundreds of hives.

Can continuous, non-invasive AI sensing reduce manual inspection and detect colony stress earlier?

01 Audio AI: hearing hive stress

Hive sound is noisy and almost never labelled. Our semi-supervised Transformer learns from both labelled and unlabelled recordings. It classifies the colony as Normal, NoQueen or Swarm, and flags stress.

02 Computer vision: seeing the queen

One queen among tens of thousands of near-identical workers is a rare-target problem, with a class imbalance of about 10,000 to 1. A data-centric pipeline makes a tiny detector work.

  • Leakage-aware splits: training and validation frames never share a source video.
  • Queen copy-paste augmentation adds synthetic queens for the rare class.
  • No GPU needed: real-time on an ordinary laptop CPU.

03 From colony to ecosystem

A PRISMA systematic review of AI-based wildfire sensing across satellite, UAV, IoT and local systems. Wildfire risk models and bee behaviour respond to the same drivers: heat, humidity, drought and smoke.

We propose bee bioacoustics as a possible fifth, complementary sensing domain for future early-warning systems.

From paper to prototype

Our prototype, running today

The research runs as a working system: a web dashboard on a laptop and a phone camera page, fed by a Raspberry Pi beside the hive.

Live from the hive

Vision

Queen detection on the live camera with YOLOv8n, on CPU only.

Acoustics

AudioMoth recordings, analysed on the Pi for dominant frequency and level.

Environment

Temperature, humidity and pressure from the hive, sent over an encrypted Tailscale link.

Technology readiness

Invention disclosure filed with AUT Ventures in June 2026. The Initial Assessment rated the prototype at TRL 3; field validation with beekeepers is the next step.
